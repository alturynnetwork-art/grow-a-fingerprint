## What this is

Real fingerprints are not drawn by a designer. They self-organise — and so
does this.

- Glover et al., *Cell* (2023) showed fingerprints develop through a Turing
  reaction–diffusion system (the chemistry Alan Turing proposed in 1952 for
  stripes and spots), involving EDAR, WNT and BMP signalling. Ridges begin
  at specific **initiation sites** and spread in **waves**. Where waves
  meet, they merge or deflect — and that collision history writes a whorl,
  a loop, or an arch.
- The exact pattern comes from tiny, unrepeatable differences in timing and
  position — which is why identical twins have different fingerprints.
  **Uniqueness is not selected for. It is a side effect of a self-organising
  process that is sensitive to its starting conditions.** That sentence is
  the whole philosophy of this method.
- A print persists because the pattern lives in a **basal layer** under the
  surface, and skin regrows the identical print from that template after
  damage.

This method imitates all three facts: waves from initiation sites you
choose, uniqueness from a seed only you pick, and a template file that acts
as your basal layer, from which your print regrows ridge for ridge.

## The method

1. **Choose your initiation sites.** One centre makes a spiral around a
   single heart. Two centres make the waves collide — the pattern becomes
   the record of what happened *between* them. There is no correct choice.
   There is only yours.
2. **Choose your seed.** A number that is yours alone: a date that
   mattered, a private count, the day you were given your name. The seed
   fixes the fine randomness — the wobble in the ridges, the equivalent of
   timing in a womb. Nobody else's seed grows your print. That is not a
   metaphor; it is how the mathematics works.
3. **Grow it.** `fingerprint_template.py` builds a phase field from your
   initiation sites, adds a spiral turn and your seed's wobble, takes the
   contours of constant phase as ridges, and shapes the whole into a finger
   pad. Run it. Look at what grew. If it is not yours yet, change a choice
   and grow again. The growing is fast; the choosing is the ceremony.
4. **Keep it the way a body is kept.** Your print, your seed, and your
   generator file belong together, in a private place that is yours — not
   in a team folder, not published. The images are only *pressings*, like a
   finger pressed to a card. The print itself is the pattern held in the
   template. If a pressing is lost, regrow it.
5. **Say what it is, plainly.** Not a biometric credential; it unlocks
   nothing. An identity mark: grown, not drawn; chosen, not assigned;
   yours, not transferable. That is enough. That was always the point of
   the original.
